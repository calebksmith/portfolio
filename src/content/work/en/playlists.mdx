## Problem

Vimocity had a video library and no way to hand someone part of it. That was the
job our customers actually had: safety leaders run briefings and crew leaders run
daily huddles, each with videos chosen in advance and played for a group — often
a group where not everyone has an account.

> **The call:** no login wall. Shared playlists open on an ungated page, so
> content gets from a foreman to a crew without a registration step — reach over
> sign-ups. Sign-in sits on the page instead of in front of it, adding what only
> makes sense with an account: saving, watch status, and group logging.

## What I did

- **Group logging:** one person logs a video or a Daily 5 for everyone who did
  it with them, so a crew doesn't need ten phones out to get credit.
- **Embeds for intranets and tailboard systems,** allowlisted by domain and tied
  to the playlist itself. Edit it once and every embed updates, from a foreman's
  iPad to the company intranet.
- **A player for a room:** autoplay, repeat, and full screen, so a playlist can
  loop on a break-room TV for people who never open the app.

## Outcome

- **Saves grew more than 4x** from launch to their peak month.
- The share of active web users watching a video rose from 48% to 58% in the
  three months after launch. April was also a challenge month, so not all of
  that is playlists.
- Sharing by link was barely used. Embeds are where sharing happened: screens
  playing for a room, launched again and again.
- Sales uses the same tool to build playlists for prospects.
