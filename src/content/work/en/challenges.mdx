## Problem

The Daily 5 is a full-body movement routine that takes five minutes or less.
Done consistently, it has been shown to help prevent injuries on the job, along
with the stiffness and soreness that physical work often causes.

Daily 5 completions are Vimocity’s key engagement metric, and one of our main
goals is helping workers make the routine part of their day, on the job and
off. So we built challenges: a layer of competition that puts teams of
coworkers head-to-head. This was 0→1. There was no challenges feature to
improve on, so the scoring, the join windows and the challenge types were all
decisions rather than inheritances.

> **Design challenge:** letting teams of very different sizes compete on a
> level playing field. We normalized leaderboard scores to completion rate
> rather than totals, so a small crew can compete against a large one. A team
> earns up to 100 points a day: the share of its members who completed the
> Daily 5.

## What I did

- **Started with research.** We talked with customers to find the main UX
  challenge: how to get more people completing the Daily 5. I then tested each
  mechanic with customers and non-customers.
- **Made it easy to compete.** Completing the Daily 5 is all it takes to take
  part in a challenge.
- **Time-boxed every challenge.** Join windows run from a week to over a month,
  so a challenge reads as an event with an end.
- **Designed two challenge types:** a leaderboard for teams and a target
  challenge for individuals. A third type, team targets, was retired after low
  participation.

### Leaderboard

Teams of coworkers compete for the top of the leaderboard, and the team on top
when it ends wins.

Leaderboards drew the highest engagement of any challenge type. Between
challenges, Daily 5 engagement dropped: competition built the routine, but
didn’t hold it.

**Who did what:** I owned design from research through UI. Engineering built
it, and we worked out the mechanics as a team.

> **What I’d change:** two things. First, the 100-point cap. Committed crews all
> max out and tie, and the tiebreaker (largest team, then a random draw) hands
> the win back to the bigger team, the bias normalizing was meant to remove. The
> next iteration makes full completion the entry price, not the max score.
> Second, bonus points for watching safety videos beyond the Daily 5, so
> challenges also grow viewership of our safety content.
