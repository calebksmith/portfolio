## Problem

The Daily 5 is a full-body routine, four or five moves in five minutes or
less, that helps prevent injuries in physical work. Completions are Vimocity’s
key engagement metric, but people struggled to make it a habit on their own.
Competition was the missing ingredient: a team up against coworkers has a
reason to do the Daily 5 on a Tuesday in February, as long as nobody is ranked
without opting in.

> **The call:** normalize leaderboard scores to completion rate, not totals.
> Totals reward headcount: the biggest team wins by default, and a small crew
> has no reason to join. So a team earns up to 100 points a day, the share of
> its members who completed the Daily 5.

## What I did

- **Started with research.** It set the brief, a daily reason without
  pressure, and I tested each mechanic against it with customers and
  non-customers.
- **Scored the existing habit.** Only a completed Daily 5 counts, so a
  challenge adds a reason, not a task.
- **Time-boxed every challenge.** Join windows run from a week to over a month,
  so a challenge reads as an event with an end.
- **Designed for competitive teams, with a way out.** Target challenges give
  everyone else, often the people the Daily 5 helps most, a prize to play for
  without a ranking.

## Outcome

- **Daily 5 completions rose 65%** among participants, and leaderboard
  challenges drew the highest engagement of the three types.
- 25% more people registered for Vimocity to join a challenge.
- Of visitors who viewed a challenge, 10% signed in within a week, against 4%
  of everyone else. Months with a major challenge bring 61% more sign-ins.
- Between challenges, Daily 5 engagement dropped: competition built the
  routine but didn’t hold it.

**Who did what:** I owned design, from research through UI. Engineering built
it, and we worked out the mechanics as a team.

> **What I’d change:** the 100-point cap. Committed crews all max out and tie,
> and the tiebreaker (largest team, then a random draw) hands the win back to
> the bigger team, the bias the percentage was meant to remove. Next iteration:
> make full completion the entry price, not the max score.
