## Problem

The Daily 5 is a full-body routine of four or five moves, done in five minutes
or less. It helps prevent injuries and keeps your body moving well for physical
work. Completions are Vimocity's key engagement metric, and the routine works
best done daily.

Engagement was inconsistent: on their own, people were less likely to make it a
habit. The missing ingredient was competition. Many of our users are
competitive, and a team up against coworkers on another team has a reason to do
the Daily 5 on a Tuesday in February. It couldn't feel like surveillance,
though, or put anyone on a leaderboard they didn't opt into.

> **The call:** normalize leaderboard scores to completion rate, not totals. Totals
> reward headcount: the biggest team wins by default, and a small crew has no
> reason to join. So a team earns up to 100 points a day, the share of its
> members who completed the Daily 5. Ten people, nine completions: 90.

## What I did

- **Started with research.** It set the brief: a reason to do the Daily 5 every
  day, without pressure. I tested each mechanic against that brief with
  customers and non-customers.
- **Built for competitive teams, with a way out.** The leaderboard puts teams
  of coworkers against each other. Target challenges give everyone else, often
  the people the Daily 5 helps most, a prize to play for without a ranking. Three
  challenge types, and only one ranks anyone.
- **Scored the existing habit.** A completed Daily 5 is the only thing that
  counts, so a challenge adds a reason to do it, not a new task.
- **Time-boxed every challenge.** Join windows run from a week to over a month,
  so a challenge reads as an event, not an obligation.

## Outcome

- **Engagement:** Daily 5 completions rose 65% among participants, and
  leaderboard challenges drew the highest engagement of the three types.
- **Adoption:** 25% more people registered for Vimocity to join a challenge.
- **Re-engagement:** 10% of visitors who viewed a challenge signed in within a
  week, against 4% of everyone else. Months with a major challenge bring 61%
  more sign-ins.
- **The catch:** outside a challenge window, Daily 5 engagement dropped.
  Competition built the routine; between challenges, it didn't hold.

> **What I'd change:** the 100-point cap. A crew where everyone completes the
> Daily 5 maxes out, so committed crews tie and the tiebreaker decides: largest
> team, then a random draw. That hands the win back to the bigger team, the bias
> the percentage was meant to remove. Next iteration: make full completion the
> entry price, not the max score.

**Who did what:** I owned design end to end, from research through UI.
Engineering built the frontend and backend, and we worked out the mechanics as a
team. Admins run challenges in [challenge admin](/work/challenge-admin), and new
participants sign up through [login and account creation](/work/login).
