## Problem

Vimocity was built for field workers and the safety leaders who support them,
but utility companies also employ desk-based workers: call centers and the
office staff who support the field. They still face stiffness, soreness, and
potential injuries from sitting all day. Our goal was to give them movement
suggestions throughout the workday, on a schedule that works for them.
This was 0→1: a new app, for a group of users Vimocity hadn’t designed for
before.

> **Design challenge:** reaching desk workers on locked-down workstations. Call
> centers restrict which programs and websites people can use, so we built a
> Windows desktop app that IT departments could approve, which meant its
> notifications would show up on those restricted machines. Some workstations
> are also shared from day to day, so sign-in had to be quick, and V-minder had
> to keep working for whoever signed in.

## What I did

- **Reused the shared sign-in.** On a shared workstation, each person signs in
  to their own schedule and preferences, through the same
  [login](/work/login) as every other platform.
- **Personalized the content.** Our biggest feedback was that not everyone can
  stand, so users choose seated moves, standing moves, or both, and can leave
  out areas with past injuries. Videos on ergonomics and office safety sit
  alongside the moves.

- **Put the schedule in the user’s hands.** They pick the days, hours, and how
  often (8 moves a day, against a recommended 6 to 8), and can play a move
  early or swap one they don’t want.
- **Connected moves to the Daily 5.** Four V-minder moves now earn Daily 5
  credit, which used to mean switching to the web portal or phone app. That
  credit counts toward any [challenge](/work/challenges) the user has joined,
  and V-minder shows it.
