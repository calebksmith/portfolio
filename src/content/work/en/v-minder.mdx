## Problem

Vimocity started with field crews, but utility companies also employ
desk-based workers: call centers, safety managers, and the office staff who
support the field. They still face stiffness, soreness, and potential injuries
from sitting all day. Our goal was to give them movement suggestions throughout
the workday, on a schedule that works for them.

> **Design challenge:** reaching desk workers on locked-down workstations. Call
> centers restrict which programs and websites people can use, so we built a
> Windows desktop app that IT departments could approve, which meant its
> notifications would show up on those restricted machines. Some workstations
> are also shared from day to day, so sign-in had to be quick, and V-minder had
> to keep working for whoever signed in.

## What I did

- **Used the same sign-in as every other platform.** V-minder signs in through
  the shared [login and account creation](/work/login) flow, so on a shared
  workstation, each person signs in to their own schedule and preferences.
- **Added content for desk work.** Users can mix in non-movement videos made
  for office workers, like setting up a more ergonomic workstation, along with
  other health and safety topics for office environments.
- **Built customization around user feedback.** The biggest feedback we heard
  was about content: some users can’t do standing moves. We added settings for
  seated moves, standing moves, or both, and later for target areas, so people
  with past injuries or restrictions can leave those areas out.

- **Let users set their own schedule.** They choose the days, the hours, and
  how often a move arrives, and see what that adds up to, like 8 moves a day,
  beside the recommended 6 to 8.
- **Let users move on their own terms.** A move can be played off schedule
  whenever someone is ready, and if the next one isn’t a move they want, they
  can swap it for another.

- **Connected V-minder to the Daily 5.** Before, the Daily 5 only counted if
  you marked it complete, or watched a Daily 5 video, in the web portal or the
  phone app. V-minder has movements rather than Daily 5 videos, so we made
  watching four of them earn Daily 5 credit, without switching over to the web
  portal. That also lets V-minder users take part in
  [challenges](/work/challenges) indirectly: V-minder shows which challenges
  they’re in, and confirms when their Daily 5 credit counts toward one.
