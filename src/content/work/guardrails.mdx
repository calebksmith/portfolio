## Problem

A design system only works if people follow it. Ours was well built but
drifting: components got used incorrectly, raw color values slipped into code,
and catching it depended on me reviewing everything. That made design the
bottleneck. AI coding tools made it worse — they generated plausible code fast,
and none of it knew our rules.

## What I did

- Wrote the component standards down in one place, covering structure, tokens,
  typing, accessibility, documentation, and versioning.
- Extended the same approach to the product itself, with page-level rules
  covering token use, accessibility requirements, loading states, and responsive
  breakpoints.
- Turned both sets of standards into automated checks that run before code
  merges, so violations are caught by the system rather than by a person.
- Loaded the same standards into our AI coding tools, so generated components
  and pages follow the rules by default.
- Kept all of it in sync from a single source, and maintain it as the system
  changes.

## Outcome

- One standard, enforced three ways: written for people, checked by automation,
  loaded for AI.
- Design stopped being a bottleneck — the full team can build production-quality
  interfaces.
- Reliance on outside design help dropped substantially.
- Prototyping opened up to people who aren't designers.

## Why it matters

Most teams are adding AI to their workflow without deciding what "correct" means
first. Writing the standard is the hard part. Enforcing it is what makes it
hold.
